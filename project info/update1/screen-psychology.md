# Screen-by-Screen Psychology — What Each Page Should Make Someone Feel

This maps every screen to its purpose, the specific feeling it should produce, the real psychological principle behind that feeling, and — because this app is built to avoid manipulation — the manipulative version of that same trigger we're deliberately *not* using. Hand this to a designer or UI generator alongside the visual prompts; tone and pacing come from this document, pixels come from the other one.

**The umbrella principle:** every trigger below is grounded in genuine value already present in the product (real routines, real community, real savings) — never manufactured (fake scarcity, fake urgency, fake randomness). If a feeling can only be produced by hiding or inventing something, it doesn't belong here — that's the line drawn in `guiding-principles.md`.

---

### 01 — Onboarding
**Purpose:** Convert curiosity into a signed-up user without demanding trust it hasn't earned yet.
**Feeling:** Relief and quiet optimism — "finally, something built to help me decide, not sell to me."
**Principle:** Reducing perceived risk before asking for commitment. The skip-friendly profile step signals autonomy (Self-Determination Theory) — the user chooses how much to invest, which increases willingness to continue rather than abandoning at the first ask.
**Avoid:** Don't manufacture urgency to sign up ("limited spots," countdown). The relief comes from *low pressure*, not high stakes.

---

### 01b — Skin Type Quiz
**Purpose:** Personalize without making someone feel diagnosed or judged for not knowing their own skin.
**Feeling:** Curiosity and mild self-discovery — "oh, that's actually true about my skin" — not clinical assessment anxiety.
**Principle:** Small, easy self-disclosure questions create a mild reciprocity/investment effect (the more someone tells you, the more invested they feel) while the immediate, easy-to-answer format keeps competence high — no question should make someone feel they're failing a test.
**Avoid:** Never let the result read as a verdict ("You have oily skin") — always an estimate they can override. A diagnostic tone would create anxiety, not curiosity.

---

### 02 — Your Shelf (Home, Returning User)
**Purpose:** Make opening the app feel like checking in on something that's *theirs*, not opening a tool.
**Feeling:** Ownership and quiet competence — small satisfaction from checking off routine steps, reassurance from real alerts, a sense of "I'm not doing this alone" from the interleaved community snippet.
**Principle:** The Zeigarnik effect (unfinished tasks stay mentally present) pulls attention back to an incomplete routine — but honestly, because the routine is real and self-chosen, not manufactured. Progress/mastery motivation (seeing 2 of 4 done) drives completion more reliably than guilt does.
**Avoid:** The streak and incomplete-routine indicators must never carry loss-framing language ("don't break your streak!"). The pull back should come from genuine incompleteness, not fear.

---

### 02b — Your Shelf (New User, Empty)
**Purpose:** Make a brand-new user feel like they've walked into a room where things are already happening, not an empty waiting area.
**Feeling:** Welcomed curiosity — "oh, people are actually talking about this stuff" — belonging *before* they've contributed anything themselves.
**Principle:** Social proof works even when it's not personalized to you yet — seeing real, specific numbers and real community voices lowers the perceived risk of engaging. This is the same instinct behind a busy restaurant feeling more inviting than an empty one, applied honestly (real counts only).
**Avoid:** Never inflate the numbers or activity shown here. A small real number ("127 tracking this") produces genuine trust; a vague large claim ("millions") produces the opposite the moment it's doubted.

---

### 03 — Search & Browse
**Purpose:** Let someone scan quickly and feel in control of a lot of information, not overwhelmed by it.
**Feeling:** Calm agency — "I can tell at a glance what's worth looking at," without decision fatigue.
**Principle:** Recognition over recall — colored match badges let the brain pattern-match instantly rather than reading and calculating. This reduces the cognitive load that normally causes choice paralysis in shopping contexts, which is itself a source of anxiety this product exists to remove.
**Avoid:** No badge should ever imply urgency ("only a few left," "trending now" in a manipulative sense) — the calm comes from clarity, not competitive pressure.

---

### 04 — Product Detail Page
**Purpose:** Walk someone from "I don't know anything about this" to "I understand this and trust what I'm seeing" — progressively, not all at once.
**Feeling:** Growing clarity and curiosity, building toward confidence — each section should make the next section feel worth opening, not exhausting to get through.
**Principle:** Ethical use of the curiosity gap — the video journey and Discovery Feed preview create a genuine "I want to see more of this" pull, resolved honestly by real content rather than clickbait. Peer authority (real owners) is psychologically more persuasive than brand claims because it bypasses the skepticism people have toward advertising.
**Avoid:** The Confidence Score must never feel like a verdict handed down from on high — it always comes with its reasoning, so the feeling is "I understand why," not "I was told what to think."

---

### 04b — Discovery Feed
**Purpose:** Let someone get pulled into browsing the way they would on Pinterest — discovery, not evaluation.
**Feeling:** Delighted immersion — "let me just see one more" — plus an underlying sense of relief that this feels *real*, including the posts that aren't glowing.
**Principle:** Genuine variety in real content (some posts enthusiastic, some critical, some just informative) creates authentic engagement — this is different from manufactured variable-reward mechanics because the unpredictability comes from real human diversity, not an algorithm designed to exploit dopamine response.
**Avoid:** This is the one place most tempting to quietly favor positive content for a "nicer" feed. Resist it — the trust payoff of visible honesty is worth more long-term than a prettier-looking feed.

---

### 05 — Store List
**Purpose:** Let someone complete a decision they've already emotionally made, without second-guessing or feeling steered.
**Feeling:** Practical calm and trust — "I'm choosing this myself, and no one gamed the order I'm seeing it in."
**Principle:** Explicitly stating "no store paid to rank higher" reduces reactance (the instinct to resist when we suspect we're being sold to) — naming the absence of manipulation directly is itself trust-building, more effective than silently hoping the user assumes fairness.
**Avoid:** No scarcity language on stock/pricing unless it's genuinely true and sourced from real data — a fabricated "only 2 left" here would undo the trust built on every screen before it.

---

### 06 — Discussion Threads (list)
**Purpose:** Let someone feel like there's a conversation already happening for their *exact* concern, not a generic pile of comments to dig through.
**Feeling:** Relief — "there's literally a thread for this."
**Principle:** Perceived relevance drives engagement far more than volume of content — a specific thread title signals care and organization, lowering the effort someone expects to spend finding their answer.
**Avoid:** Don't let thread titles become clickbait-y ("You WON'T BELIEVE...") — the relief comes from genuine specificity, not manufactured intrigue.

---

### 06b — Discussion Thread (detail)
**Purpose:** Make someone feel accompanied in their decision — like they're not the only one who's ever wondered this — while being able to tell what's genuinely theirs to trust.
**Feeling:** Validation ("someone else asked exactly this") layered with quiet intellectual trust from being able to see what's a real reply versus AI-surfaced versus borrowed content.
**Principle:** Social validation reduces the isolation of a purchase decision. Separately, transparency about content sourcing builds what's sometimes called epistemic trust — people trust a source more, not less, when it's honest about the limits and origins of what it's showing them.
**Avoid:** Never let the AI-surfaced "related" content or YouTube bootstrap content blur into looking like a genuine reply — the moment someone feels tricked about what's real here, the validation flips into suspicion.

---

### 07 — Routine Tracker
**Purpose:** Make a daily/twice-daily habit feel satisfying to complete, not like a chore to track.
**Feeling:** Small, repeated competence and self-consistency pride — "I'm someone who takes care of this" — with zero anxiety about imperfection.
**Principle:** Real habit formation is driven by the satisfaction of completion (a genuine, if small, dopamine response to finishing a real task), not by fear of losing a number. Self-compassion research consistently shows that gentle, non-punitive framing sustains habits longer than guilt-based framing, which tends to cause people to abandon a habit entirely after one missed day ("I already broke it, why bother").
**Avoid:** This is the screen most apps get wrong with loss-averse streak mechanics. The copy and visuals must stay in "pick up where you left off" territory, never "you lost your streak."

---

### 08 — Progress Journal
**Purpose:** Give someone a private space to notice their own change over time, with sharing as a genuine choice, not an expectation.
**Feeling:** Quiet ownership over one's own story, and — when they do choose to share — authentic pride rather than performative posting.
**Principle:** The IKEA effect — people value things more when they've built them themselves; a self-documented journey feels more meaningful than a generic review. Privacy-by-default also creates the psychological safety needed for someone to be honest with themselves first, which paradoxically makes what they eventually choose to share more genuine and more valuable to others.
**Avoid:** No pressure language nudging someone to share more or more often — the sharing itself should feel like the user's own idea each time, never a prompt-driven obligation.

---

### 09 — Verified Owner Profile
**Purpose:** Make genuine contribution feel like it built something real — specific expertise, not internet points.
**Feeling:** Earned pride and recognition — "I'm someone people trust about oily skin specifically" — a sense of identity, not just a score.
**Principle:** Self-Determination Theory's competence and relatedness needs are met far more durably by specific, domain-bound recognition than by generic leaderboard status — being "known for" something taps identity in a way a global point count never does, and identity-based motivation sustains contribution longer than status-chasing does.
**Avoid:** No ranking against other users, no "#3 top contributor" framing — that reintroduces status competition, which tends to degrade into gaming the system for points rather than genuinely helping.

---

### 09b — The Satchel (collection view)
**Purpose:** Remove the anxiety of "I have to decide on this right now or I'll lose track of it."
**Feeling:** Playful relief and a sense of being organized — a small delight from the mascot/animation, plus the practical comfort of "nothing here is going to slip through the cracks."
**Principle:** Externalizing memory (writing things down, or in this case, collecting them visually) measurably reduces the cognitive load of holding multiple open decisions in mind at once — this is why to-do lists work at all. The added delight of the mascot interaction taps positive affect, which broadens attention and makes browsing feel lighter rather than effortful (this is real, well-established affect research, not manipulation, because the delight isn't attached to any dark pattern — it's just a nice interaction wrapped around a genuinely useful function).
**Avoid:** The Satchel should never create artificial pressure to check out ("items expiring from your Satchel soon!") — its entire value proposition is removing pressure, not adding a new kind.

---

### 09c — Satchel Carried Into In-App Store View
**Purpose:** Prevent the feeling of being "cut loose" the moment someone leaves the app to actually buy something.
**Feeling:** Continuity and quiet reassurance — "I'm still supported here, even though I've technically left."
**Principle:** Context continuity reduces the anxiety associated with transitioning between trusted and untrusted environments — seeing a familiar, friendly element persist across that boundary keeps the emotional register consistent instead of an abrupt tonal drop into an unfamiliar store page.
**Avoid:** The floating widget shouldn't nag or interrupt the external store's own flow (no pop-ups, no motion that distracts from completing the purchase) — its job is reassurance, not intrusion.

---

### 10 — Saved & Price Alerts
**Purpose:** Make someone feel looked after between visits, without ever feeling watched or pressured.
**Feeling:** Pleasant, low-key surprise — "oh nice, it actually dropped" — and a background sense of being taken care of.
**Principle:** Genuine, real-data-driven surprise (an actual price drop, not a manufactured one) produces authentic positive reinforcement for having saved something in the first place, encouraging the behavior without any manipulation, because the reward is materially real, not designed to trigger a compulsive check-in pattern.
**Avoid:** Never simulate a price drop or invent urgency around one. Alert frequency should stay genuinely tied to real price movement, never padded out to increase how often the app pings someone.

## Using This Alongside the Visual Prompts

The `ui-generator-prompts.md` document tells a generator or designer *what a screen looks like*. This document tells them *why it should feel that way* — pacing, copy tone, and the restraint required on screens like the Discovery Feed (04b) and Routine Tracker (07), where the manipulative version of the same feature is a very short step away from the honest one. When in doubt on a specific line of copy or interaction detail, the test is simple: **would this feeling survive the user finding out exactly how it was produced?** If yes, it's earned. If the feeling depends on them not noticing something, it doesn't belong in this product.
